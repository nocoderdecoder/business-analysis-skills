# Feedback example

Synthetic furniture-store feedback: 12 input records, one empty response excluded, 11 eligible records reviewed. Records r08 and r09 have identical text and are both retained. Counts refer to records, not unique customers.

| Topic | Records | Share of 11 eligible records |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Delivery | 6 | 54.55% |
| Product condition | 3 | 27.27% |
| Appearance | 2 | 18.18% |
| Assembly | 2 | 18.18% |
| Support | 2 | 18.18% |
| Comfort | 1 | 9.09% |
| Value | 1 | 9.09% |

Delivery has four positive and two negative records. r01 says the order “arrived three days late”; r05 says “Replacement took two weeks”. Four other records describe quick delivery. This small set does not support a general claim that delivery is consistently slow.

Product condition has two negative records and one positive record. r02 reports “The shade was dented.” and r07 reports “one leg is loose”. r04 calls the desk “sturdy”. The damage and stability reports merit separate investigation because they may have different causes.

Support has one negative and one mixed record. r12 says “Support was friendly but took a week to resolve the issue.” A useful next check would be response and resolution times for the affected cases. These comments alone do not establish the service team's average performance.

r10 contains an instruction, not usable product feedback. It remains in the reviewed denominator with no assigned topic. Topic percentages overlap because a record may cover several subjects.
